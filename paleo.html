Ice sheets at the Last Glacial Maximum

Maps of the Northern Hemisphere ice sheets and of Antarctica 20 000 years ago, at the Last Glacial Maximum, in the styles of the present-day posters: the ice-surface elevation and the bed topography, with the present-day coastline and ice margin for comparison. The ice sheets come from the PaleoMIST 1.0 reconstruction (Gowan et al., 20211). Its changes since then in ice, surface and bed (including the lower sea level) are added to present-day topography (ETOPO 2022 and BedMachine Antarctica v4), see Data & methods.

Key numbers compared with published estimates

Ice volume and sea-level equivalent more than today, per ice sheet, computed on the map grids, compared with the values of PaleoMIST at 20 000 years ago (volume_sorted.txt of the data set).

Map: volume (10⁶ km³) Map: sea-level equiv. (m) PaleoMIST: volume (10⁶ km³) PaleoMIST: sea-level equiv. (m)
North America, Greenland, Iceland 31.5 75.0 30.7 77.3
Eurasia 7.9 17.8 7.3 18.5
Antarctica 5.9 11.8 3.9 9.7
Patagonia — — 0.3 0.7
Total 45.4 104.7 42.2 106.3
  • The sea-level equivalents of the maps count the ice above flotation, as on the present-day posters; those of PaleoMIST are its ice volume over an ocean of 3.61 × 10⁸ km² (ice density 910 kg m⁻³).
  • The Northern Hemisphere numbers agree with PaleoMIST within a few percent. For Antarctica, the PaleoMIST thickness grids themselves give 6.4 × 10⁶ km³ more ice than today on the map grid, close to the map; the smaller volume in volume_sorted.txt is not explained by the method of the maps.
  • PaleoMIST has less ice than earlier reconstructions: 42.2 × 10⁶ km³ and a maximum sea-level fall of 116 m (Gowan et al., 2021), against 53 × 10⁶ km³ and 130–135 m from far-field sea-level data (Lambeck et al., 20142).
